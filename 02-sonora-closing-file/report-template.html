Finding 03

Beneficiary of record and substitution

What the file shows

The trust agreement dated [date] names [name] as beneficiary. [State whether any assignment exists, whether it is public or private, and whether the trustee bank issued an amendment.]

What that means in practice

Until the trustee bank issues the substitution and it is recorded, the bank's records show the prior party as beneficiary. That party — not you — can instruct the trustee, sell, encumber and designate who inherits. Your private assignment is a valid contract against a person; it is not a change of record.

What to do, and when

Do not release funds until the trustee issues the beneficiary substitution and the corresponding filing is made. The documents required are [list], and ordinary trustee turnaround is [N] weeks. Build that time into your closing date rather than into your regrets.

Verified against

Trust agreement, assignment instrument, trustee statement, registry certificate.

Not verified

The trustee's internal file, which is not accessible to a third party.

Critical — stop the closing

Finding 04

Estate: what happens when you die

What the file shows

The substitute beneficiary designation in your trust is [blank / names [person] / names [person] with a spelling discrepancy]. You [do / do not] hold a Mexican will covering assets situated in Mexico.

What that means in practice

With a valid substitute beneficiary designation, the transfer of trust rights on death is administrative: the trustee recognizes the substitute on presentation of the death certificate and identity documents. Without it, your heirs open a Mexican succession proceeding for the property — a court process, in Spanish, taking years, with Mexican counsel paid throughout, before anyone can sell or even list it.

What to do, and when

Complete the designation before signing, spelled exactly as the passport spells it, with a first and second substitute. If the trust is already executed, the amendment costs [amount] and takes [N] weeks. There is no cheaper moment than the one you are in.

Verified against

Trust agreement, identity documents provided, any will furnished.

Not verified

The interaction with your U.S. estate plan, which your U.S. attorney must confirm. Coordination note attached.

Critical — fix before signing
