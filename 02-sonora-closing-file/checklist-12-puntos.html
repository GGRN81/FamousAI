The 12-Point
Closing File Checklist

Print this. Take it to your closing. Ask for each item by name and write down who answered. If someone cannot answer a line, that line is your finding.

01 · Restricted zone
Is the property inside the restricted zone — within 50 km of the coast or 100 km of a border? If yes, a foreign national cannot hold direct title and needs a bank trust or a Mexican company.
02 · Owner of record
Does the name on the public registry certificate match the person selling to you, exactly? Not the listing agent's word. The certificate.
03 · Chain of title
Ask for the three prior transfers. Every gap, every missing recording, every heir who never signed, is your problem after you fund.
04 · Ejido origin
Was this land ever communal agrarian property, and was it properly regularized? Unregularized ejido land cannot be conveyed to a foreign national.
05 · Trust term
When does the bank trust expire, and whose responsibility is renewal? Get the answer in writing from the trustee bank, not from the seller.
06 · Beneficiary substitution
If you are buying rights under an existing trust, has the trustee bank issued the substitution? A private assignment is not enough.
07 · Substitute beneficiary
Is the substitute beneficiary line filled in, with the right person, spelled the way their passport spells it? This is the most skipped line in the document.
08 · Permit scope
Does the Foreign Affairs permit describe the use you actually intend — residence, rental, commercial? A mismatch surfaces later.
09 · Vehicle
Bank trust or Mexican company? The answer depends on how many properties, whether you will rent, and how you plan to exit. It is decided once.
10 · Encumbrances
Liens, mortgages, property tax certificate, water certificate, condominium regime, HOA dues and reserve fund status.
11 · Exit position
Do you have a Mexican tax ID, and will you need a designated legal representative when you sell? This can only be arranged while you still own it.
12 · The gaps
List what your closing packet does NOT contain. That list is usually shorter to write and far more expensive than the list of what it does.

Reverse side

Six questions to ask out loud

Ask these at the table, in front of everyone, and write down the answers with the name of who gave them. The value is not the answer. It is that the question was asked on the record.

1. May I see the registry certificate, dated this month?
Not a copy from the file. A current one. If it is older than 30 days, it is describing the past.
2. Who is the beneficiary of record at the trustee bank, today?
Ask for the bank's own statement, not the seller's description of it.
3. What name goes on the substitute beneficiary line, and who confirms the spelling?
Passport spelling. If it is blank at signing, it stays blank for years.
4. When does the trust term end, and who files the renewal?
"The bank handles it" is not an answer. Ask which department and on what notice.
5. What does the permit say the property is for?
If you plan to rent and the permit says residence, you want to know now.
6. What am I going to need on the day I sell that I cannot get later?
This is the question nobody asks and the one that decides whether the investment worked.
