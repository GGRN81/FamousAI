# Email sequence — checklist to report

Four emails, 48 hours apart, triggered by the free checklist download. **The first three sell
nothing.** Each one narrates a single failure mode.

Register: direct, consequence-first, no Latinate sentence structure. No exclamation points, no
"Hi there!", no fabricated urgency. Every `[____]` is yours to fill; the cases are composite,
figures shifted, minimum 18-month lag, never a client.

## Email 2 — Day 2

**Subject:** The line almost nobody fills in

> Your Mexican bank trust has a line for a substitute beneficiary. It is one line. It is
> usually blank.
>
> Filled in correctly, the transfer on your death is administrative: the trustee recognizes
> the substitute on presentation of a death certificate and identity documents. Weeks.
>
> Blank, your heirs open a Mexican succession proceeding for a beach house. A court process,
> in Spanish, taking years, with Mexican counsel paid the whole time, before anyone can sell
> the property or even list it.
>
> The name has to be spelled the way the passport spells it. Name a second substitute too.
>
> It takes four minutes at signing. I have watched families spend [____] and [____] years
> because nobody used those four minutes.
>
> Gilberto

## Email 3 — Day 4

**Subject:** Your trust may still be in the seller's name

> If you bought rights under an existing trust, ask one question: did the trustee bank issue
> the substitution?
>
> Many Sonora transactions are documented with a private assignment — signed, witnessed, a
> copy for each side. It is a valid contract. It is not a change of record.
>
> Until the bank issues the amendment, the bank's records show the prior party as beneficiary.
> That person can instruct the trustee, sell, encumber, and decide who inherits. You hold a
> contract against someone who may no longer be findable.
>
> This is usually discovered years later, by the buyer, at the moment they try to sell.
>
> Ask the trustee bank directly for a statement of who the beneficiary of record is today.
> Not the seller. The bank.
>
> Gilberto

## After

**Bought** → 10 days after delivery, one email: *"Anything in the report you want me to walk
through?"* Nothing else. If they are moving forward, they will say so.

**Did not buy** → one touch every 30–45 days, and every touch delivers something new. Never
*"did you get a chance to look at what I sent?"* — that is a collection call wearing a
question mark, it produces guilt, and guilt produces silence.

**The realtor** → the real channel. After every report, one email to the buyer's agent with
the two generic lessons from the file, stripped of any identifying fact. No pitch. Agents who
receive that twice start sending clients.
